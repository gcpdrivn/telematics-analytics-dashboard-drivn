0. Telemetry & Statistical Foundations

Core sampling rules, filtering logic & mathematical definitions
Foundational Architectural Philosophy: Commercial fleet telemetry raw data is subject to sensor dropouts, GPS drift, depot yard holding, and mid-period asset commissioning. Rather than applying naive calendar-day arithmetic, the Drivn analytics engine enforces Active-Day Normalization, Bessel-corrected Relative Dispersion, and Tenure-Adjusted Dynamic Denominators to maintain absolute statistical and operational veracity.
Daily Distance Measurement & Odometer Differentiation $d_{i,t}$
Primary Signal Physical Unit: km
Mathematical Formulation
$$d_{i,t} = \mathrm{ODO}_{i,t,\text{close}} - \mathrm{ODO}_{i,t,\text{open}} = \int_{t_0}^{t_1} v_i(\tau) \, d\tau$$
VariableData TypeDescription
$d_{i,t}$Float ($\text{km}$)Net distance traversed by vehicle $i$ during dispatch cycle date $t$.
$\mathrm{ODO}_{i,t,\text{close}}$Float ($\text{km}$)Closing cumulative wheel odometer reported by telematics gateway at end-of-day ($23:59:59$).
$\mathrm{ODO}_{i,t,\text{open}}$Float ($\text{km}$)Opening odometer reported at start-of-day ($00:00:00$).
$v_i(\tau)$Float ($\text{km/h}$)Instantaneous velocity telemetry stream sampled at $1\text{ Hz}$ to $0.1\text{ Hz}$.
🧠 Physical & Telematics Intuition
High-frequency GPS tracking can suffer from "telematics drift" (accumulating spurious meters while parked under high-rises or inside depot structures). Drivn utilizes closing and opening CAN-bus wheel odometer registers as the primary source of truth, verifying GPS integrated velocity $\int v(\tau) d\tau$ only for tamper and disconnect detection.
💼 Commercial Fleet Use Cases
  • Corridor Tolling & Trip Invoicing: Eliminates billing disputes with corporate clients by guaranteeing true wheel-rotation mileage.
  • Lease Contract Overage Auditing: Verifies that monthly mileage ceilings (e.g. $15,000\text{ km/month}$) are tracked with zero cumulative drift error.
The Active Operating Day Filter $\mathbf{1}(d_{i,t} > 0)$
Filtering Paradigm Discrete Indicator
Mathematical Definition
$$A_i = \sum_{t=1}^{T_i} \mathbf{1}(d_{i,t} > 0), \quad \text{where } \mathbf{1}(P) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } P \text{ is true} \\ 0 & \text{if } P \text{ is false} \end{cases}$$
🧠 Physical & Statistical Intuition
Why separate active days from calendar days? If a bus operates $600\text{ km/day}$ on 20 days and sits in scheduled depot maintenance for 10 days, its calendar-day average is $400\text{ km/day}$. However, its battery and powertrain never experienced a $400\text{ km}$ day! They experienced $600\text{ km}$ duty cycles. Conflating idle days with running days hides true shift intensity and misleads battery degradation modeling.
💼 Commercial Fleet Use Cases
  • Duty-Cycle Energy Sizing: Sizing depot DC fast chargers according to active shift consumption rather than artificially diluted calendar averages.
  • Driver Shift Rostering: Ensuring active crew schedules comply with maximum daily driving limits without distortion from off-duty days.

🧠 Mathematical & Operational Intuition
Notice the crucial dual reporting:
1. Macro Daily Throughput ($\bar{D}$): Measures overall commercial business volume. If half the fleet is parked, macro throughput drops by 50%.
2. Micro Vehicle Utilization ($\bar{d}_{\text{active}}$): Isolates machine workload when in motion. If only 5 buses run, but they run $700\text{ km}$ each, micro utilization is high ($700\text{ km/d}$) while macro throughput is low ($3,500\text{ km/d}$).
💼 Commercial Fleet Use Cases
  • Utility Power Contract Sizing: Depot electricity demand tariffs (kVA demand charges) are priced on aggregate fleet distance per day.
  • Carbon Credit Monetization: Total fleet distance directly converts to verified emissions avoidance under clean mobility credit frameworks.
  • Asset Revenue Per Kilometer (RPK): Multiplying $\bar{D}$ by freight/passenger rate provides daily revenue run-rates.

🧠 Statistical Intuition: The Tenure-Adjustment Principle
A major statistical pitfall in fleet telemetry is survivor and onboarding bias. BillionE's 20 trucks were progressively onboarded throughout August: some gave their first telemetry ping on 14-Aug, 17-Aug, or 20-Aug. If we blindly assumed all trucks had 30 eligible days, the denominator would be $600\text{ days}$, producing an artificially depressed availability of $\frac{440}{600} = 73.3\%$. By dynamically computing each truck's calendar tenure from its first live transmission date $T_i = (T_{\text{end}} - T_{\text{first}, i}) + 1$, the true eligible denominator is $511\text{ days}$, revealing an actual operational availability of $86.1\%$.
💼 Commercial Fleet Use Cases
  • OEM Warranty Availability SLAs: Contractual guarantee validation (e.g. OEM agreement stipulating $>90\%$ vehicle availability).
  • Ramp-Up Diagnostics: Distinguishing between mechanical downtime versus assets that simply have not yet been delivered to the customer.

Daily KM Volatility Percentage & Dispersion Tiering $\mathrm{CV}_i \text{ \& } \overline{\mathrm{CV}}_{\text{cust}}$
Volatility Index Dimensionless (%)
Mathematical Formulation
$$s_i = \sqrt{\frac{1}{A_i - 1} \sum_{t=1, d_{i,t}>0}^{T_i} (d_{i,t} - \bar{d}_i)^2}, \quad \mathrm{CV}_i = \left( \frac{s_i}{\bar{d}_i} \right) \times 100\%$$ $$\overline{\mathrm{CV}}_{\text{cust}} = \frac{1}{|V_c|} \sum_{i \in V_c} \mathrm{CV}_i, \quad \bar{s}_{\text{cust}} = \frac{1}{|V_c|} \sum_{i \in V_c} s_i$$
Volatility TierMathematical ThresholdColor EncodingOperational Manifestation
Stable$$\mathrm{CV} < 25.0\%$$vol-lowScheduled fixed timetable corridor. Distance identical day-in, day-out ($\sigma \approx \pm 100-140\text{ km}$).
Moderate$$25.0\% \le \mathrm{CV} < 50.0\%$$vol-midFixed routes with weekend surges, mountain route relief rotation, or traffic variance.
Volatile$$\mathrm{CV} \ge 50.0\%$$vol-highOn-demand industrial freight, loading dock queues, short local hops mixed with long hauls.
🧠 Statistical Intuition: Why Coefficient of Variation (CV)?
If Bus A averages $600\text{ km/day}$ with a standard deviation of $\pm 120\text{ km}$, and Truck B averages $200\text{ km/day}$ with $\pm 120\text{ km}$, they have identical raw standard deviations ($s = 120\text{ km}$), but radically different dispatch stability!
• For Bus A: $\text{CV} = \frac{120}{600} = 20\%$ (Highly predictable intercity schedule).
• For Truck B: $\text{CV} = \frac{120}{200} = 60\%$ (Completely erratic, tripling or dropping to zero).
The Coefficient of Variation normalizes scale, providing an unbiased metric of route stability.
💼 Commercial Fleet Use Cases
  • Depot Charging Slot Reservation: Stable fleets ($\text{CV} < 25\%$) enable rigid automated charging schedules. High volatility fleets require dynamic opportunity chargers.
  • Driver Compensation Planning: Eliminates salary disparity caused by fluctuating trip assignments by identifying routes with high coefficient of variation.

🧠 Physical & Battery Duty Intuition
Commercial electric vehicle range is bounded by battery pack energy density ($200\text{–}300\text{ kWh}$ packs deliver approximately $200\text{–}300\text{ km}$ on a single charge). • $1\text{–}200\text{ km}$: Single-shift operation; overnight depot AC/DC charge only.

• $401\text{–}600\text{ km}$: Extended line-haul corridor; requires 2 intermediate opportunity fast charges.

  • En-Route Fast Charging Infrastructure Sizing: Correlating high-percentage days in $>600\text{ km}$ ranges across the fleet quantifies peak daily demand for high-power DC fast charging hubs.
